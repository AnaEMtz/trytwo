Following the money and the ships

Evasion is a game of concealment

Sanctioned actors rarely act in their own name. Public reports describe a recurring pattern: hide who is behind a deal, hide where goods came from, and hide where money ends up.

  • Layered companies that obscure the real owner
  • Vessels that switch off tracking or change identity
  • Goods routed through third countries with altered paperwork
  • Funds moved through intermediaries and, increasingly, digital assets

Why Panels of Experts matter

The UN has no enforcement agency of its own. Panels supply the independent evidence that gives sanctions teeth.

Evidence gathering. Experts trace vessels, companies, financial flows and weapons, drawing on field work, open sources, satellite imagery and information from governments.
Practical recommendations. Reports suggest new designations, closing legal loopholes and steps for governments, shippers and banks to reduce exposure.
A changing landscape. The Panel of Experts supporting the DPRK sanctions committee ended its work on 30 April 2024 after a Security Council vote to renew it was vetoed. In response, a group of states launched the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team to keep reporting on violations. See the timeline →
